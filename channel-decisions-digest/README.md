## The outcome

A week of conversation becomes one page you can act on. The decisions the channel settled sit at the top, each with the person who made it, so nobody has to scroll back to find out whether the venue, the supplier or the release date was agreed or only discussed. Ideas that were floated and never settled are listed apart, quoted, so a "probably" never hardens into a decision by accident.

Below them come the questions nobody has answered yet, with who asked and who was asked, and every promise made in the channel. A promise with a named owner and a date sits apart from one that says "we'll sort it soon", so the gaps are plain. Anything left open last week comes forward with what happened to it since. Every line ends with the message id and time it came from, so anyone can check it in seconds.

The digest is about the work, never the people. It never counts who writes the most or says who is slow to reply. The AI assistant drafts it; the channel's owner checks it, chases what needs chasing and decides whether to share it.

## Who starts the work, and when

The person who owns the channel starts it: a team lead, a chief of staff, a project lead or an account lead. They run it weekly, and again ahead of a handover, a holiday cover or a project review, so that whoever comes next can see what the channel has settled and what it is still waiting on. They read the draft against the messages, take each item flagged for follow-up to the people concerned, and decide where the checked digest goes.

## What they supply

- **The channel export from your team chat tool**, for one channel and the period you choose, with the columns message_id, timestamp, author, thread_id and text. If your tool names them differently, the assistant stops and asks which is which before it reads anything.
- **A people list:** each name as it appears in the export, with that person's role, and which senders post automatically.
- **The settings:** the channel's name, the first and last dates of the period, today's date, and how many days a question may stay open before it is flagged.
- **Optionally, last period's digest,** so open items are carried forward.

Nothing is connected to the chat tool, and no password is ever asked for. Direct messages and private conversations are never part of it.

## What comes out

A dated digest in a fixed order:

1. **To raise:** the items worth following up, each with what to find out.
2. **Decisions made,** with who made them, and who reported them when someone else decided.
3. **Not counted as decisions:** proposals that were never settled, quoted.
4. **Questions still open,** with who asked, who was asked and how many days each has waited.
5. **Promises with an owner and a date,** each marked open, reported done or past its date.
6. **Promises missing an owner or a date,** in the words used.
7. **Carried from the last digest,** each with what became of it.

A count of messages on personal matters, left out, closes it. Every line cites its message id and timestamp. The digest reads to the period's last date only: a promise due on that date is shown as open and is not flagged, and a promise is flagged as past its date only when the date falls before the period's last date.

## What has to change for your company

Choose the channels worth a digest and the day it runs. Decide which channels may be exported at all, and keep direct and private messages out. Keep the default columns or tell the assistant yours. Keep the people list current, including the senders that post automatically. Set how long a question may wait before it is flagged. Name the personal subjects that stay out of any digest, and where those conversations are held instead. If your team settles things with words of its own, such as "signed off" or "locked", add them. Agree who may read the digest once it has been checked.
